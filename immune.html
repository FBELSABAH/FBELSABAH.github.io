The same clues can mean different errors.

A middle-qubit flip and two outside flips give the same parity syndrome in the ideal code. The decoder assumes a single error. It repairs the first case and applies the wrong repair in the second.

Mask order: data0, data1, data2. Syndrome order: Z0Z1, Z1Z2. “Return” is a selected-input inverse-preparation check—not complete state fidelity. A |+⟩ input can conceal a logical-X failure.

Correction has overhead.

At the recorded 10% independent-flip setting, the ideal bank has lower protected failure. In the noisy bank, protected failure is higher than the bare reference. The extra circuit operations can erase the benefit.

What the code protects.

An established three-qubit bit-flip code protects against a single X error. It does not protect against arbitrary phase errors or multiple bit flips, and it is not a new quantum algorithm.

Three data qubits and two syndrome ancillas form the teaching example. The complete 13-qubit experiment bank also includes selectors, noise environments and a bare reference.
