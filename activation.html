Activation Gradient Flow

A small neural network trained on noisy XOR provides a controlled way to compare four activation functions. Changing the starting weight scale changes the outcome enough that one activation ranking is not a dependable conclusion.

NumPy · initialization · multi-seed experiments · December 2025 – April 2026

Collaborative project with Mohamed Elsabah. A shallow network and a synthetic noisy-XOR task—not a comparison of deep networks on real-world data.

The question

Does the best activation stay the same when the initial weight scale changes?

At scale 0.5, mean final test accuracy was 90.67% for ReLU, 75.73% for tanh, 70.00% for arctan and 86.40% for softsign.

How it was tested.

  • From-scratch shallow multilayer perceptron in Python and NumPy.

  • ReLU, tanh, arctan and softsign; Xavier/Glorot scales 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0.

  • Ten random seeds per setting; 3,000 epochs in the documented final sweep.

  • Compare accuracy with pre-activation, derivative and layerwise-gradient diagnostics.

What to take from it.

  • At scale 2.0, all four mean final test accuracies were between 91.07% and 91.20%.

  • Similar final accuracy did not make the internal derivative diagnostics identical. The first-percentile absolute derivative for tanh approaches zero in the scale-2 figure, while arctan and softsign remain higher.

Scope and limitations.

  • A shallow network on a small synthetic noisy-XOR task; these results do not establish the best activation for deep networks or real-world datasets.

  • The shaded seed variation is not a confidence interval.

  • Derivative-tail diagnostics describe activation derivatives and should not be presented as a proof of healthy or failed gradient flow throughout the network.
