Treatment Thresholds

A reproducible simulation explores how an eligibility cutoff trades off expected benefit, treatment cost and a stylized screening cost across 10,000 synthetic patients.

Python · sensitivity analysis · visualization · May 2026
Source code ↗

Synthetic model, not clinical guidance. Benefit and cost use abstract units, not dollars or observed patient outcomes. The screening-cost formula is intentionally stylized.

The question

How does a threshold-dependent screening-cost assumption change the best tested treatment cutoff?

Across the tested grid, the maximizing threshold falls from 0.20 to 0.05 as the screening-cost parameter rises from 0.01 to 0.30.

How it was tested.

  • Generate 10,000 benefit probabilities from Beta(2, 5), with NumPy random seed 42.

  • Treat a synthetic patient when its benefit probability is at least the threshold.

  • Test 19 thresholds from 0.05 to 0.95 against five screening-cost settings: 95 combinations.

  • Treatment cost is 0.20 per treated patient. Screening cost equals 10,000 × screening-cost parameter × threshold.

  • Net system value equals expected total benefit minus treatment cost minus screening cost.

What to take from it.

  • That direction follows from the chosen screening-cost formula: a higher threshold itself increases screening cost.

  • The calculation illustrates sensitivity to assumptions. It is not a recommendation to expand or restrict treatment in a real health system.

Scope and limitations.

  • Synthetic probabilities, not patient records or a clinically validated risk model.

  • Expected benefit is the sum of probabilities; no realized patient outcomes are simulated.

  • The screening-cost relationship is stylized and drives the direction of the result.

  • No uncertainty intervals, subgroup analysis, clinical resource constraints or externally validated policy effect.

  • Best tested cutoff means the maximum on the documented discrete grid, not an unconstrained continuous optimum.
